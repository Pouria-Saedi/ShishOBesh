The Timber

Every board begins as rough timber.

We choose wood the way a luthier chooses spruce — by ear, by grain, by weight in the hand. Four timbers make it into the workshop. Everything else is turned away.

Walnut

Warm and straight-grained, forgiving to carve. The base of every board we make.

Rosewood

Dense, dark, and faintly fragrant. Reserved for the points, where contrast matters most.

Maple

Pale and tight-grained. Cut thin for inlay, where precision has to show.

Ebony

Rare and unforgiving to work. Used sparingly, and only on the Reserve edition.

The Process

Hand-Carved. Hand-Planed.

In the workshop — every point is chiselled by hand.

Each point is chiselled by hand, not routed — the small variations are left in, not sanded away. Every face is planed until it's smooth, then planed once more. A single board takes the better part of a week to move from rough timber to finished table, and no two boards leave the bench quite the same.

The wood remembers every year it grew. We only reveal it. — from the workshop

The Workshop
